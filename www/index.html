About This Monitor

Bitcoin mining evidence, including work outside the active chain

Explore Bitcoin mining through child-chain proofs, Bitcoin Core observations and recovered records: pool attribution, stale and orphan evidence, and consensus-invalid error blocks. Coinbase protocol declarations and cited Findings add context to that record.

  1. 1. Blocks outside the active chain

    Competing valid blocks can form branches. The active chain keeps one branch; others become stale, including blocks displaced by a reorganisation. The active chain alone does not preserve the whole mining record.

  2. 2. Merge mining stamps a copy

    Merge-mined chains reuse Bitcoin's proof of work. A merge-mined child block carries a merge-mining proof embedding the exact Bitcoin header the miner was hashing.

  3. 3. Proof of work is not full validity

    A child chain applies its own rules, so it can preserve proof of a Bitcoin template that meets the target but violates a Bitcoin consensus rule. These are error blocks, distinct from stale blocks. A header or proof alone does not validate every transaction in the Bitcoin block.

  4. 4. Classify from the available evidence

    Core context establishes chain placement; live checks and a reviewed catalogue identify known consensus failures. Strict orphan evidence uses an authenticated coinbase height; weak evidence relies on timestamp and difficulty-epoch agreement. Both require Core absence and lineage checks. Incomplete evidence can remain unknown.

Origins & credit

Based on research by Nicholas Stifter (SBA Research, Vienna) and colleagues in Echoes of the Past: Recovering Blockchain Metrics From Merged Mining (2018) and Merged Mining: Curse or Cure? (2017).

For invalid-block evidence, see bitcoin-data/invalid-blocks and Invalid Bitcoin blocks: full PoW, consensus invalid. This monitor uses a pinned Research mirror of reviewed errors, not the whole upstream catalogue or its unproven reported cases.
